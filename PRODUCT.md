# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Curious adults playing a one-minute daily ritual on their phone, sometimes on desktop. They know the daily-puzzle genre, like animals, and share results in a group chat. One attempt per day, so they want to squint, commit, and enjoy the reveal.

## Product Purpose
Close Call is a daily guessing game: name the animal from a photo taken far too close. Every wrong guess — or a deliberate zoom-out — pulls the camera back a step. Success is a player who comes back tomorrow, learns one surprising thing (the Fact), and posts a result that is instantly recognisable as Close Call rather than "another Wordle clone".

## Brand Personality
A field camera, mid-focus. Precise, patient, a little wry — the viewfinder corners, the zoom readout, the ladder of magnifications. Naturalist's equipment rather than naturalist's prose.

## Anti-references
- Wordle and its clones: the green/yellow/gray tile palette, `#121213` near-black, flip-tile reveal, pill share button.
- Stock-photo nature branding: leaf motifs, earth-tone gradients, handwritten "wild" scripts.
- Quiz-show styling: timers, score multipliers, confetti.

## Design Principles
1. **The photograph is the interface.** Everything else is camera furniture around it; nothing competes with the image.
2. **Zooming out is the clock.** The Zoom ladder shows, at a glance, how much room is left — before the player reads a single word.
3. **Meaning beyond colour.** Tries read through position and label as well as hue, in the app and in the share text.
4. **One reveal moment.** Motion is saved for the zoom between Tries and the final pull-back; everything else is quick and quiet.
5. **Teach something.** The Fact is the reward; give it a place of honour.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA, verified by axe in the e2e suite. Full keyboard play, including the suggestion combobox, and screen-reader announcements after every Try. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Results never rely on colour alone; an optional high-contrast mode is available. Light, dark and system themes.
